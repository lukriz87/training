Audiometry Training Simulator

Practice presenting tones, recording simulated patient responses, and interpreting an audiogram.

Educational use only. This is not a calibrated audiometer or a medical diagnostic tool. Browser audio cannot produce verified dB HL levels; the displayed levels and patient responses are simulated.

1. Select a case

Training task
Estimate the lowest level at which the simulated patient responds “heard” at each frequency. Reveal the case afterward to compare your work.

2. Present a tone

Present a tone to begin.

The tone is optional and played at a safe, low browser volume. The simulated response is determined by the selected case, not by your actual hearing.

3. Your audiogram

Use “Record estimate at this level” to place a point for the selected ear and frequency. The point is your current estimate, not the hidden case threshold.

Learning notes

  • Audiograms conventionally place frequency on the horizontal axis and hearing level on the vertical axis, with more hearing loss lower on the chart.
  • Air-conduction thresholds alone do not establish the type or cause of a hearing loss. Clinical assessment may also use bone conduction, masking, and other tests.
  • In practice, threshold-finding protocols use repeated responses and carefully controlled equipment. This simulator simplifies that process for learning.
